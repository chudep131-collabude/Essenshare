About This Platform

A free, nonprofit-operated digital infrastructure connecting donors of surplus food and useful products with recipients and distribution partners worldwide.

Our Mission

"No good food should become waste while someone nearby is hungry."

Surplus food is not a scarcity problem — it is a distribution problem. Every day, tons of good food are discarded while people nearby go without. This platform exists to close that gap, permanently and at scale, without bureaucracy or barriers.

We do not ask recipients to prove their need. We do not require accounts to browse or collect. We build globally, with dignity at the centre of every design decision.

How the Platform Works

For Donors

Individuals, businesses, restaurants, bakeries, farms, and organisations register a free donor account and post surplus food or useful products. Each listing includes safety information, allergen details (where applicable), a pickup location, and a pickup deadline. Donors must agree to our Donor Safety Agreement before posting.

When a recipient arrives, the donor confirms the handover using a temporary single-use code. Inventory is reduced atomically — no over-allocation, no guesswork.

For Recipients

Anyone can browse available donations without creating an account. Search by location and category. Request a single-use pickup code. Travel to the donor's location and collect. No documentation of need, no registration, no barriers.

For Distribution Partners

Food banks, shelters, NGOs, and community organisations register as distribution partners to receive and redistribute donations at scale. Partners are verified and approved before gaining access to bulk allocation features.

Platform Principles

These 22 principles are non-negotiable. They define how the platform operates and how every design decision is made.

  1. Recipients do not need an account to find or receive donations.
  2. Recipients are never asked to prove poverty or need.
  3. Donors may be individuals or organisations/businesses.
  4. Every donor must provide a verified pickup location.
  5. Businesses do not upload registration documents during normal onboarding.
  6. Business verification uses publicly available information where practical.
  7. Donors must agree not to distribute unsafe food or products.
  8. Donors are responsible for accurate donation descriptions.
  9. Only confirmed donor handovers reduce inventory.
  10. Recipients cannot manually claim inventory.
  11. Pickup codes and QR tokens are temporary and single-use.
  12. Successful pickup creates a 4-hour same-donor cooldown for that recipient.
  13. The cooldown starts after confirmed handover, not at code generation.
  14. Recipients may use a different donor during cooldown.
  15. Recipient location must remain private.
  16. Donor pickup locations must be publicly visible.
  17. Impact statistics come only from completed transactions.
  18. Safety reports must create an immutable audit trail.
  19. Do not collect unnecessary personal information.
  20. Build globally from the beginning.
  21. The platform must work on mobile and slow/poor connections.
  22. No AI features unless explicitly requested.

Operating Model

This platform is operated as a nonprofit. It is free to use for all donors and recipients. There are no premium tiers, advertising placements, or data sales. Operating costs are covered through grants, institutional partnerships, and voluntary contributions.

The platform does not derive revenue from the redistribution of food or products. All donation transactions are zero-cost to all parties.

Platform Liability Statement

The platform makes no warranty regarding the safety of any food or product listed by donors. Specifically, the platform:

  • Facilitates connections between donors and recipients;
  • Requires donors to agree to the Donor Safety Agreement before posting;
  • Operates a reporting system to remove unsafe listings promptly;
  • Is not a food retailer or distributor;
  • Does not inspect food before redistribution.

Donors bear sole responsibility for the accuracy of their listings and the safety of items they choose to distribute. This statement is included in the platform Terms of Service.

Good Samaritan Law Reference

In many jurisdictions, food donors are protected from civil liability when donating food in good faith. Examples include:

  • USA: Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act (1996)
  • UK: No specific act, but covered under common law principles
  • EU, Canada, Australia: Similar protections exist in various jurisdictions

The platform encourages donors to donate surplus rather than discard it, while maintaining the responsibility for accurate and honest listings. The platform recommends donors consult local Good Samaritan laws. The platform does not provide legal advice.
